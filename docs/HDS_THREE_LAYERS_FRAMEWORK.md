# HDS: Three Layers of Human Development

## The central model

HDS develops a person across three relationships:

1. **Self — how I lead myself and face challenges.**
2. **World — how I function in the world and environment, even when no other person is there.**
3. **Other people — how I relate, cooperate, compete, protect, lead, and deal with other people.**

Every competency in all three layers is developed through three integrated dimensions:

- **Mental (מנטלי):** knowledge, understanding, attention, perception, emotional regulation, judgment, strategy, learning, and decision-making.
- **Physical and motor (פיזי־מוטורי):** strength, endurance, speed, coordination, mobility, dexterity, movement, technical execution, and practical physical capacity.
- **Values (ערכי):** principles, character, responsibility, integrity, courage, respect, fairness, discipline, care, and ethical judgment.

These are not three additional layers. They are lenses applied to every competency. Not every competency requires all three dimensions equally: for example, navigation may emphasize mental and practical skills, while combat sports involve all three. HDS should specify the relevant dimensions and how each is assessed.

HDS is about capability, agency, adaptability, and responsibility—not domination, invulnerability, or perfection.

## Layer 1 — Self-Mastery: Me מול עצמי

**Core question:** Can I lead myself, overcome challenges, develop excellence, and act according to my chosen values even when it is difficult?

### Domains

- **Self-leadership:** personal responsibility, initiative, self-management, direction, priorities, and leading by example.
- **Facing challenges and adversity:** coping with difficulty, uncertainty, discomfort, setbacks, failure, pressure, and fear; persistence and adaptive coping.
- **Mental skills:** attention, concentration, emotional regulation, stress management, confidence, self-efficacy, resilience, motivation, and self-awareness.
- **Self-discipline and behavior:** habits, consistency, impulse management, delayed gratification, planning, time management, and execution.
- **Excellence and mastery:** deliberate practice, setting standards, receiving feedback, learning from mistakes, craftsmanship, and continuous improvement without perfectionism.
- **Physical development:** strength, power, endurance, speed, mobility, flexibility, coordination, balance, and general athletic capability.
- **Health and self-care:** nutrition, sleep, recovery, hygiene, injury prevention, and sustainable routines.
- **Learning and thinking:** memory, focus, critical thinking, creativity, metacognition, learning how to learn, and sound decision-making.
- **Identity and values:** purpose, meaning, personal principles, integrity, courage, humility, accountability, and aligning actions with values.

Examples of development methods include strength training, running, swimming, calisthenics, mixed-modal conditioning, martial arts practice, mobility work, deliberate skill practice, reflective exercises, and evidence-informed psychological interventions.

## Layer 2 — Environmental Mastery: Me מול העולם

**Core question:** If I had to operate independently in the world, without relying on another person, could I understand my surroundings, move through them, learn, solve problems, and stay safe?

This layer focuses on the person and the physical, natural, built, and informational environment. A skill belongs here when its primary challenge is interacting with the environment or a system, rather than relating to another person.

### Domains

- **Orientation and navigation:** maps, compass, GPS, route planning, landmarks, terrain reading, wayfinding, and navigating unfamiliar areas.
- **Survival and self-sufficiency:** water, shelter, warmth, fire safety, basic food procurement knowledge, signaling, resource use, and surviving environmental exposure.
- **Driving and vehicle competence:** road rules, hazard perception, defensive driving, vehicle control within one's licence and training, trip planning, basic maintenance, vehicle checks, and understanding mechanical limitations.
- **Off-road and terrain mobility:** understanding terrain, vehicle suitability, safe route choice, and supervised lawful off-road driving.
- **Outdoor and expedition skills:** hiking, camping, climbing basics, rope awareness, weather interpretation, packing, preparation, and environmental stewardship.
- **Water competence and safety:** swimming for personal safety, water awareness, currents, open-water hazards, and safe decision-making near or in water.
- **First aid and emergency response:** recognizing emergencies, basic first aid, emergency communication, personal preparedness, and knowing when professional help is required.
- **Practical and technical skills:** tools, repairs, construction basics, equipment use, maintenance, and solving practical problems.
- **Environmental awareness:** weather, geography, natural hazards, plants and animals, climate, and human-made infrastructure.
- **Technology and information literacy:** using digital tools, cybersecurity and privacy hygiene, evaluating information, and navigating technological systems.
- **Independent learning:** finding reliable information, learning unfamiliar practical skills, testing understanding, and adapting to new systems.
- **Risk and uncertainty:** recognizing hazards, estimating risk, choosing safe options, contingency planning, and knowing when to stop or retreat.
- **Everyday independence:** managing personal resources, money, transport, appointments, and essential tasks in the surrounding world.

Training must be lawful and safety-led. Driving requires qualified instruction and appropriate licensing. First aid, climbing, water safety, and survival skills should be practiced with qualified instructors and suitable safeguards. HDS must distinguish knowledge from demonstrated practical competence.

## Layer 3 — Social Mastery: Me מול אנשים

**Core question:** Can I understand other people and social situations, build healthy relationships, cooperate, compete, protect myself and others appropriately, and exercise influence and leadership responsibly?

### Domains

- **Communication:** listening, speaking clearly, asking questions, non-verbal communication, public speaking, feedback, and difficult conversations.
- **Friendship and belonging:** making friends, maintaining friendships, trust, loyalty, mutual support, community, and social participation.
- **Trust and relationships:** building and repairing trust, reliability, honesty, boundaries, vulnerability, intimacy, and recognizing breaches of trust.
- **Conflict, hostility, and betrayal:** disagreement, rejection, aggression, bullying, betrayal, manipulation, reconciliation, and deciding when to disengage.
- **Empathy and social understanding:** perspective-taking, reading social context, understanding motives and emotions, and respecting differences.
- **Leadership of people:** leading teams, setting direction, responsibility for others, delegation, mentoring, group culture, accountability, and decision-making.
- **Cooperation and teamwork:** coordination, collaboration, shared goals, mutual reliance, and resolving group problems.
- **Negotiation and persuasion:** interests, bargaining, influence, rhetoric, persuasion, compromise, and principled negotiation.
- **Manipulation and social defense:** recognizing deception, coercion, exploitation, gaslighting, pressure tactics, and manipulation; protecting boundaries and responding ethically. HDS studies these phenomena to understand and resist harm, not to teach exploitation.
- **Social strategy and game theory:** incentives, competition, cooperation, signaling, reputation, power dynamics, and strategic interaction.
- **Combat and martial arts:** boxing, kickboxing, wrestling, judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, MMA, and other structured disciplines. Assess technical skill, composure, control, sportsmanship, consent, and safety—not aggression.
- **Self-defense and protection:** awareness of interpersonal danger, avoidance, de-escalation, escape, protecting others where safe and lawful, and proportionate lawful defense.
- **Social ethics:** respect, fairness, justice, responsibility, courage, care, honesty, and the ethical use of influence and power.
- **Group and cultural competence:** understanding norms, roles, institutions, culture, community dynamics, and functioning across different social settings.

Combat training may also develop Layer 1 capacities such as discipline, fitness, courage, and emotional regulation. Its interaction with an opponent, partner, or aggressor is classified in Layer 3. The same activity can therefore contribute to multiple competencies, but each outcome must be defined and assessed separately.

NLP and other influence systems should be evaluated claim-by-claim. HDS must not treat a label, technique, or popular theory as scientifically established without adequate evidence.

## The three dimensions applied to every layer

| Dimension | What HDS examines |
|---|---|
| **Mental** | Knowledge, perception, attention, emotional regulation, judgment, strategy, learning, and decision-making. |
| **Physical and motor** | Strength, endurance, speed, coordination, mobility, dexterity, movement, and practical execution. |
| **Values** | Character, ethics, responsibility, integrity, courage, respect, fairness, discipline, and care. |

Examples:
- **Self-leadership:** mental — planning and self-regulation; physical — carrying out demanding routines; values — responsibility and integrity.
- **Navigation:** mental — map reading and decisions; physical — moving safely across terrain; values — responsibility for personal safety and the environment.
- **Leadership of people:** mental — judgment and communication; physical — presence and practical action where relevant; values — fairness, accountability, and care for the team.
- **Martial arts:** mental — tactics and composure; physical — technique and conditioning; values — restraint, respect, discipline, and sportsmanship.

A competency can have one, two, or all three dimensions. HDS should not force an artificial physical component onto purely cognitive or relational competencies.

## Cross-layer foundations

These support all three layers:

- Critical thinking and scientific literacy.
- Learning how to learn.
- Adaptability and problem solving.
- Risk assessment and decision-making.
- Ethical reasoning and responsibility.
- Reflection, feedback, and continuous improvement.
- Communication of uncertainty and limitations.

## HDS organizational and operating model — three parts

The three human-development layers describe **what** HDS develops. The following three parts describe **how HDS operates**. They are complementary structures, not competing classifications.

### 1. Human Gym — mental, values, and character development

A practical development environment for strengthening the human capacities that shape how a person thinks, responds, chooses, and behaves.

Core areas:
- Mental and psychological skills: attention, emotional regulation, resilience, confidence, stress management, and self-awareness.
- Values and ethical development: integrity, responsibility, respect, courage, fairness, and care.
- Character and behavior: discipline, perseverance, initiative, humility, reliability, and habits.
- Self-leadership: goal setting, accountability, reflection, and acting consistently with one's principles.

The Human Gym develops and trains these capacities through structured exercises, deliberate practice, reflection, feedback, and suitable assessments. Physical training can be integrated where it supports a defined mental or character-development objective, but claims about psychological transfer must be measured rather than assumed.

### 2. Development Programs — training plans, workshops, and pathways

The applied delivery arm that turns HDS knowledge and frameworks into structured development experiences.

Formats may include:
- Individual development and training plans.
- Group programs and guided pathways.
- Workshops, seminars, and practical courses.
- Short challenges and structured practice cycles.
- Programs for schools, teams, organizations, and communities.
- Integrated pathways combining mental, physical-motor, and values development across the three human-development layers.

Each program should define its target population, objectives, competencies, duration, activities, prerequisites, safety requirements, assessment methods, and expected outcomes. Programs should be adapted to the participant and should not promise outcomes beyond the supporting evidence.

### 3. Research Lab — scientific research and evaluation

The knowledge-generating and quality-control arm of HDS.

Core functions:
- Review and synthesize scientific literature.
- Define research questions and testable hypotheses.
- Develop and refine models, measurement tools, and interventions.
- Design and conduct ethical studies and pilot projects.
- Measure program outcomes and evaluate effectiveness.
- Analyze results, limitations, uncertainty, and unintended effects.
- Publish transparent reports and update HDS practices when evidence changes.

Research involving people must follow appropriate ethics review, informed consent, privacy protection, and applicable law. HDS must distinguish exploratory pilots from controlled studies and must not describe an intervention as proven based only on testimonials or uncontrolled observations.

### How the three parts work together

1. **Research Lab** investigates human development, evaluates evidence, and tests methods.
2. **Human Gym** turns suitable findings into trainable exercises and development practices.
3. **Development Programs** organize those practices into pathways and deliver them to individuals and groups.
4. Program outcomes and participant feedback return to the **Research Lab** for evaluation and improvement.

This creates a continuous learning cycle: research → practice design → program delivery → measurement → improved research.

### The two HDS frameworks together

- **Operating structure:** Human Gym · Development Programs · Research Lab.
- **Development framework:** Self-Mastery · Environmental Mastery · Social Mastery.
- **Development dimensions:** Mental · Physical-Motor · Values.

Every program can be mapped to one or more development layers and dimensions, and its methods can be linked to the evidence and evaluations maintained by the Research Lab.

## HDS research and evidence standard

Every method, intervention, and claim evaluated by HDS should be labeled:

- **Established:** supported by a sufficiently strong and convergent body of evidence for the specific claim.
- **Promising / preliminary:** some supportive evidence exists, but confidence, replication, or generalizability is limited.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposition not yet adequately established.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned explanation of evidence, distinct from the observed result.
- **Opinion / value judgment:** a normative or personal position, not an empirical finding.

Record source provenance, study design, population, limitations, uncertainty, conflicting findings, and review date. Distinguish whether a method works, for whom, under what conditions, and compared with what alternative. Avoid presenting values as scientific facts; research can study their effects, but the choice of values also involves ethical reasoning.

## Development and assessment model

Each competency should have:

1. An operational definition.
2. A baseline assessment appropriate to the competency.
3. A target and rationale.
4. A learning or training plan.
5. Relevant mental, physical-motor, and values dimensions.
6. Safety prerequisites and constraints.
7. A repeatable assessment method.
8. A record of results, context, and uncertainty.
9. A review date and next development step.

Use observable performance where possible. Physical tests, practical field scenarios, driving qualifications, supervised driving assessments, martial-arts skill assessments, communication role-play, and negotiation exercises require different assessment methods. Do not collapse them into one score without a validated rationale.

## Suggested competency maturity levels

- **Foundation:** understands basic principles and performs simple tasks with guidance.
- **Developing:** performs common tasks independently in familiar conditions.
- **Proficient:** performs reliably across varied conditions and can explain decisions.
- **Advanced:** adapts effectively to complex conditions and can help others develop.
- **Expert:** demonstrates sustained, independently verified performance in a defined specialty.

A person has a profile across competencies, layers, and dimensions—not one universal score or global label such as "elite."

## Product architecture implications

The three layers should be the primary navigation and classification structure of HDS. Each layer contains domains; each domain contains competencies. Each competency can be connected to its relevant mental, physical-motor, and values dimensions, assessments, interventions, and evidence.

Recommended entities:

- **Layer** — Self, World, or Other People.
- **Domain** — a major capability area within a layer.
- **Competency** — a specific capability with an operational definition.
- **Dimension** — mental, physical-motor, or values.
- **Assessment** — a test, rubric, or practical evaluation.
- **Target** — a user-specific development objective.
- **Intervention** — a training, learning, or behavior-change method.
- **Evidence record** — sources, findings, confidence, limitations, and provenance.
- **Development plan** — selected targets, interventions, schedule, and review points.
- **Progress record** — dated results and reflections.

The same competency, intervention, or activity may relate to more than one layer or dimension. HDS should record the specific relationship and outcome rather than assume transfer.

## Initial HDS principle

**Lead yourself. Navigate the world. Relate to people.**

HDS develops the whole person through the integration of mental capabilities, physical and motor capabilities, and values across all three layers.

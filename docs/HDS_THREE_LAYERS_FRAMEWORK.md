# HDS: Three-Layer Human Development Framework

## Purpose

Human Development Science (HDS) develops a person's capacity to act effectively, responsibly, and adaptively across three domains:

1. **Self-Mastery** — governing and developing oneself.
2. **Environmental Mastery** — understanding and operating safely and effectively in one's physical environment.
3. **Social Mastery** — understanding, communicating, and cooperating with other people, including in situations of disagreement and negotiation.

The framework is about capability, agency, adaptability, and responsibility. "Mastery" does not mean domination or total control. It means developing skills, making informed decisions, and responding constructively to uncertainty and limits.

## Layer 1 — Self-Mastery

**Core question:** Can I understand, regulate, develop, and reliably direct my own behavior and capabilities?

### Domains

- **Psychological skills:** self-awareness, emotional regulation, attention, stress management, resilience, self-efficacy, values, and reflective practice.
- **Self-regulation and behavior:** self-discipline, habits, planning, goal setting, consistency, impulse management, and behavior change.
- **Physical capability:** strength, power, muscular endurance, aerobic and anaerobic capacity, speed, mobility, flexibility, coordination, balance, and general athleticism.
- **Health and recovery:** nutrition, sleep, recovery, injury prevention, and sustainable lifestyle practices.
- **Personal learning:** deliberate practice, feedback, skill acquisition, and metacognition.

Examples of physical practice may include resistance training, running, swimming, calisthenics, CrossFit-style mixed-modal training, HYROX-style conditioning, capoeira, and mobility work. These are practice methods, not separate top-level layers.

## Layer 2 — Environmental Mastery

**Core question:** Can I understand the environment, anticipate hazards, adapt, and act effectively within it?

### Domains

- **Outdoor competence:** navigation, route planning, terrain awareness, weather awareness, and expedition preparation.
- **Survival and preparedness:** water, shelter, warmth, signaling, first aid, emergency planning, and risk management.
- **Movement and transport:** safe and responsible driving, vehicle awareness, and other relevant modes of transport.
- **Practical problem solving:** tool use, resource management, situational awareness, and adapting plans when conditions change.
- **Decision-making under uncertainty:** identifying hazards, assessing options, managing risk, and knowing when not to act.

Training must be lawful, safety-led, and appropriate to the learner's experience. Driving competence must be developed through qualified instruction and legal licensing; survival and first-aid skills require practical training with qualified providers.

## Layer 3 — Social Mastery

**Core question:** Can I understand other people, communicate clearly, build trust, cooperate, negotiate, and manage conflict ethically?

### Domains

- **Communication:** listening, asking questions, clarity, feedback, non-verbal communication, and adapting communication to context.
- **Social and emotional competence:** empathy, perspective-taking, emotional awareness, boundaries, and relationship skills.
- **Cooperation and leadership:** teamwork, coordination, accountability, group decision-making, and leadership.
- **Negotiation and conflict:** interests, options, trade-offs, de-escalation, mediation, and principled negotiation.
- **Strategic interaction:** game theory, incentives, signaling, cooperation, competition, and decision-making in interdependent situations.
- **Influence and persuasion:** rhetoric, ethical persuasion, critical evaluation of influence techniques, and resistance to manipulation.
- **NLP:** treated as a collection of claims and techniques to be evaluated individually. HDS must not present NLP as an established scientific theory or assume its broad efficacy without strong supporting evidence.

## Cross-layer foundations

The following capabilities support all three layers rather than belonging exclusively to one:

- Critical thinking and scientific literacy.
- Ethical reasoning and responsibility.
- Risk assessment and decision-making.
- Learning how to learn.
- Adaptability and problem solving.
- Reflection, feedback, and continuous improvement.

## HDS research and evidence standard

Every method, intervention, and claim evaluated by HDS should be labeled according to its evidential status:

- **Established:** supported by a sufficiently strong and convergent body of evidence for the specific claim.
- **Promising / preliminary:** some supportive evidence exists, but confidence, replication, or generalizability is limited.
- **Hypothesis:** a testable proposition that has not yet been adequately established.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned explanation of evidence, distinct from the observed result.
- **Opinion / value judgment:** a normative or personal position, not an empirical finding.

HDS should record source provenance, study design, population, limitations, uncertainty, conflicting findings, and the date of review. It should distinguish evidence that a method works from evidence about who it works for, under what conditions, and compared with what alternative.

## Development and assessment model

Each competency should have:

1. A clear operational definition.
2. A baseline assessment appropriate to the competency.
3. A target level and rationale.
4. A learning or training plan.
5. Safety constraints and prerequisites where relevant.
6. A repeatable assessment method.
7. A record of results, context, and uncertainty.
8. A review date and next development step.

Assessment should use observable performance where possible, not motivation or self-description alone. Physical tests, practical field scenarios, driving qualifications, communication role-play, and negotiation exercises require different assessment methods and must not be collapsed into one score without a validated rationale.

## Suggested maturity levels

Use descriptive levels, not a single universal ranking:

- **Foundation:** understands basic principles and performs simple tasks with guidance.
- **Developing:** performs common tasks independently in familiar conditions.
- **Proficient:** performs reliably across varied conditions and can explain decisions.
- **Advanced:** adapts effectively to complex conditions and can help others develop.
- **Expert:** demonstrates sustained, independently verified performance in a defined specialty.

A person can be at different levels in different competencies. HDS should show a profile across the three layers rather than label a person globally as "elite."

## Product architecture implications

The three layers should become the primary navigation and classification structure of HDS.

Recommended core entities:

- **Layer** — one of the three top-level domains.
- **Domain** — a major capability area within a layer.
- **Competency** — a specific capability with an operational definition.
- **Assessment** — a test, rubric, or practical evaluation.
- **Target** — a user-specific measurable development objective.
- **Intervention** — a training, learning, or behavior-change method.
- **Evidence record** — sources, findings, confidence, limitations, and provenance.
- **Development plan** — selected targets, interventions, schedule, and review points.
- **Progress record** — dated results and reflections.

Research should be linked to the relevant competency and intervention. The same intervention may support multiple competencies or layers, but HDS should record the specific mechanism and outcome rather than assume transfer.

## Initial HDS principle

**Develop the self. Navigate the environment. Work effectively with people.**

The three layers form one integrated model of human capability, while retaining distinct definitions, evidence standards, and assessment methods.

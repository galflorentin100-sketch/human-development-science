# HDS Mental Capability Map — v1.0

**Status:** Proposed research taxonomy for organizing questions. It is not a validated scale, a diagnostic instrument, or a claim that these categories are scientifically independent.  
**Scope:** Initial map of mental and behavioral capabilities. Social and environmental contexts are cross-cutting; physical-motor capabilities are mapped separately in the wider HDS framework.

## 1. Why this map exists

HDS needs a structured inventory of what it intends to study. Without one, terms such as "mental toughness", "discipline", "resilience", "confidence", and "grit" can become vague labels that combine different mechanisms and outcomes.

The map is a navigation system for research, not a final theory of the mind. Existing constructs may overlap, sit at different explanatory levels, or be measured in incompatible ways. HDS must preserve those differences until evidence supports integration.

## 2. The eight provisional capability families

### M1. Self-awareness and metacognition
**Working meaning:** noticing and interpreting one's own internal state, thinking, behavior, strengths, limits, and errors.

Candidate components:
- Awareness of attention, thoughts, emotions, bodily state, and impulses
- Monitoring progress and detecting errors
- Metacognitive knowledge: knowing what one knows and does not know
- Reflection and updating one's self-understanding
- Accurate calibration of confidence and uncertainty

Do not confuse:
- Self-awareness with self-esteem.
- Confidence with accuracy.
- Reflection with rumination.
- Self-report with demonstrated insight.

Possible observations: calibration between confidence and actual performance; error detection; task-specific monitoring accuracy; structured reflection. Any tool must be checked for reliability and validity.

### M2. Executive control and action regulation
**Working meaning:** coordinating attention, working memory, inhibition, switching, planning, and monitoring to support goal-directed action.

Candidate components:
- Sustaining and shifting attention
- Inhibiting a prepotent or inappropriate response
- Working-memory updating
- Cognitive flexibility and task switching
- Planning, sequencing, and monitoring action
- Initiating, stopping, and adjusting behavior

Do not treat executive functions as a single muscle or assume that a laboratory task directly measures everyday discipline. Executive-function definitions and task measures vary across research traditions.

Possible observations: validated task measures, task completion and error patterns, goal-maintenance behavior, and ecologically valid performance in everyday tasks.

### M3. Goal formation, motivation, and persistence
**Working meaning:** selecting meaningful goals, generating and sustaining goal-directed effort, and adjusting effort when conditions change.

Candidate components:
- Goal choice and prioritization
- Intrinsic and extrinsic motivation
- Expectancy, perceived value, and self-efficacy
- Commitment and implementation intentions
- Persistence and effort allocation
- Goal disengagement and re-engagement when appropriate

Do not confuse:
- Persistence with continuing at any cost.
- Motivation with capability.
- Self-efficacy (belief about capability) with actual capability.
- Grit or conscientiousness with a directly trainable skill without evidence.

Possible observations: goal adherence over time, effort allocation, task initiation latency, persistence under defined conditions, and calibration of perceived versus actual capability.

### M4. Emotion regulation and coping
**Working meaning:** processes through which people influence which emotions they have, when they have them, and how they experience or express them.

Candidate components:
- Emotion identification and differentiation
- Understanding triggers and appraisals
- Reappraisal and perspective change
- Acceptance and willingness to experience discomfort
- Modulation of emotional intensity and expression
- Recovery after emotional activation
- Selecting coping strategies appropriate to controllability and context

Do not define regulation as suppressing emotion or always becoming calm. Strategy effectiveness is context-dependent. Coping and emotion regulation overlap but are not interchangeable; measurement reviews identify conceptual and measurement inconsistencies.

Possible observations: strategy selection in context, recovery trajectories, behavioral functioning during emotional activation, validated self-report, and observer/task measures. Avoid treating one physiological signal as a direct readout of a specific emotion.

### M5. Psychological flexibility and adaptation
**Working meaning:** responding to changing internal and external conditions in ways that remain workable and aligned with relevant goals or values.

Candidate components:
- Cognitive flexibility and perspective shifting
- Behavioral repertoire and strategy switching
- Tolerance of uncertainty
- Adapting plans when feedback changes
- Acting in the presence of difficult thoughts or feelings
- Balancing consistency with responsiveness

Do not collapse psychological flexibility, cognitive flexibility, adaptability, and resilience into one construct. Reviews report many overlapping definitions and measures; construct boundaries remain contested.

Possible observations: switching strategies when conditions change, performance under changing rules, recovery of effective action after disruption, and context-specific validated measures.

### M6. Stress response, resilience, and recovery
**Working meaning:** patterns of responding, adapting, recovering, and seeking support in the face of significant challenge or adversity.

Candidate components:
- Anticipatory preparation and risk appraisal
- Response regulation during stress
- Recovery after acute challenge
- Adaptation over repeated or prolonged demands
- Maintaining or restoring functioning
- Help-seeking, support use, and resource mobilization

Important boundary:
Resilience is not a fixed personality badge, invulnerability, or proof that adversity is beneficial. It can describe a process, a capacity, or an outcome; these must be specified separately. Context and available resources matter.

Possible observations: change in functioning over time, recovery trajectory, adaptive behavior under defined stressors, support seeking, and validated context-specific measures. Never infer resilience from willingness to endure unsafe conditions.

### M7. Learning, skill acquisition, and transfer
**Working meaning:** acquiring, retaining, retrieving, and applying knowledge and skills across time and contexts.

Candidate components:
- Attention to instruction and encoding
- Retrieval practice and feedback use
- Error-based learning and adjustment
- Deliberate practice and skill refinement
- Retention after a delay
- Generalization and transfer to untrained situations
- Learning from observation and social feedback

Do not equate improvement on the practiced exercise with general capability. Measure retention and transfer separately.

Possible observations: acquisition curves, delayed retention tests, performance on untrained tasks, feedback responsiveness, and real-world application.

### M8. Judgment, decision-making, and problem-solving
**Working meaning:** selecting and revising actions using available information, goals, uncertainty, and consequences.

Candidate components:
- Problem definition and decomposition
- Information search and source evaluation
- Critical and creative thinking
- Risk and probability judgment
- Decision-making under time pressure or uncertainty
- Generating and comparing alternatives
- Monitoring outcomes and learning from decisions

Do not assume that confidence, speed, or a successful outcome proves good judgment. Outcomes are affected by chance and information availability.

Possible observations: decision quality against pre-defined criteria, calibration, information search quality, reasoning transparency, and performance across repeated decisions.

## 3. Cross-cutting contexts

Every capability should be studied in more than one context where appropriate:

- **Self-context:** alone, habits, private choices, personal goals.
- **Physical/environmental context:** fatigue, heat/cold, navigation, uncertainty, time pressure, unfamiliar tasks, safety constraints.
- **Interpersonal context:** cooperation, conflict, leadership, social evaluation, trust, peer pressure, communication.
- **Digital/information context:** distraction, online information, technology-mediated decisions, misinformation.
- **Everyday life context:** work, education, family, health routines, finances, and community participation.

A context is not automatically a stress test. Define its demands and safety limits.

## 4. Distinguish the level of explanation

For each research question, tag the target as one or more of the following:

1. **Process/mechanism:** e.g. attentional inhibition, appraisal, working-memory updating.
2. **Skill/strategy:** e.g. planning a task, using reappraisal, seeking feedback.
3. **Belief/perception:** e.g. self-efficacy, perceived control, confidence.
4. **Disposition/tendency:** e.g. trait conscientiousness or habitual persistence.
5. **Behavior:** what the person actually does in a specified situation.
6. **Outcome:** e.g. task completion, wellbeing, safety, learning, or sustained functioning.

Do not combine these into a single score without a defensible measurement model and validation evidence.

## 5. A standard capability research card

Every capability page or study should contain:

- ID and version
- Preferred construct name and synonyms
- Definition and explicit exclusions
- Level(s) of explanation from section 4
- Candidate subcomponents
- Related and potentially overlapping constructs
- Proposed mechanisms and competing explanations
- Contexts and target populations
- Observable indicators
- Candidate measures and psychometric evidence
- Training/intervention candidates and their evidence status
- Transfer and retention tests
- Safety, accessibility, and equity considerations
- Open questions and known disagreements
- Source list, search date, and reviewer status

## 6. Initial priority order for HDS research

This is a proposed order based on strategic coherence and feasibility, not a ranking of human importance.

**First research cluster**
1. M2 Executive control and action regulation
2. M3 Goal formation, motivation, and persistence
3. M4 Emotion regulation and coping
4. M7 Learning, skill acquisition, and transfer

**Second research cluster**
5. M1 Self-awareness and metacognition
6. M5 Psychological flexibility and adaptation
7. M6 Stress response, resilience, and recovery
8. M8 Judgment, decision-making, and problem-solving

The first cluster is a practical starting point for investigating the knowledge–capability–performance gap. The second cluster should not be treated as less important; it is sequenced to avoid trying to research every broad construct simultaneously.

## 7. First flagship research question

**When people know a useful strategy and can demonstrate it in a low-pressure setting, what predicts whether they will use it consistently in real-life situations with competing demands?**

Candidate explanatory factors:
- Strategy knowledge and task-specific skill
- Executive control and attention
- Motivation, goal value, and self-efficacy
- Emotional activation and coping strategy
- Habit strength and environmental cues
- Fatigue, health state, and available resources
- Social context and perceived consequences
- Feedback, planning, and opportunity

Possible study sequence:
1. Map existing constructs and measures before inventing an HDS-specific scale.
2. Conduct a focused literature review and identify competing models.
3. Select a narrow behavior and population.
4. Measure knowledge, demonstrated capability, and repeated real-world behavior separately.
5. Examine contextual predictors prospectively.
6. Only then design and test an intervention.
7. Assess retention and transfer beyond the trained setting.

This question is a candidate research program, not a claim that a new mechanism has been discovered.

## 8. Evidence anchors and cautions

The following sources support the need for construct separation and careful measurement; they do not validate this HDS taxonomy:

- A 2024 scoping review comparing coping and emotion-regulation measurement reports substantial conceptual overlap and unresolved measurement alignment.
- A systematic review of self-regulation measures in children and adolescents identified cognitive, behavioral, and emotional components and found that no single reviewed measure covered all components.
- A scoping review of psychological flexibility identified many overlapping constructs and raised concerns about definitional and measurement problems.
- A review of life-skills frameworks shows recurring domains such as decision-making, problem-solving, critical thinking, communication, empathy, coping, and stress management.

These sources should be checked in full and expanded through a reproducible search before any formal review or evidence grading.

## 9. Non-negotiable scientific rule

HDS may develop its own organizing language, but it must not rename established constructs and present them as discoveries. Where existing science already has a useful construct, HDS should map to it, compare definitions, and identify the genuine unresolved question. A new HDS label is justified only when it adds explanatory or measurement value and survives independent review.

# HDS Research Protocol — Self-Discipline

Status: PRE-REGISTRATION / RESEARCH SPECIFICATION
Scientific state: NO FINDINGS YET

## Primary research question

What is self-discipline, what constructs or measurable processes comprise it, and what evidence supports ways of developing it?

## Scientific constraints

HDS must not assume a single accepted definition of self-discipline.
The research must first map competing definitions and operationalizations.

Every output must be classified as:
- FACT: directly supported by verified evidence.
- INFERENCE: evidence-grounded interpretation that does not exceed the evidence.
- HYPOTHESIS: testable proposition not yet established.
- OPINION: value judgment or preference.

No study, sample, statistic, effect, citation, mechanism, or intervention may be invented.
Correlation must not be presented as causation.
Unknown or insufficient evidence must remain explicit.
Contradictory evidence must be preserved and surfaced.

## Research subquestions

1. Definition
   - How is self-discipline defined across major scientific traditions?
   - Which definitions overlap, and where do they differ?
   - Is self-discipline treated as a trait, state, ability, behavior, self-regulatory process, or a combination?

2. Composition
   - What measurable constructs are empirically associated with self-discipline?
   - How does self-discipline relate to executive function, inhibitory control, delay of gratification, goal pursuit, habit formation, motivation, effort regulation, emotion regulation, conscientiousness, and related constructs?
   - Which proposed components are empirically distinguishable rather than merely different names for the same construct?

3. Measurement
   - Which validated instruments or behavioral tasks measure self-discipline or closely related constructs?
   - What evidence exists for reliability, validity, predictive validity, and behavioral correspondence?
   - Where do self-report and behavioral measures disagree?

4. Development
   - What interventions have been tested to improve self-discipline or its component processes?
   - Which study designs provide causal evidence?
   - What effects, if any, have been observed on the trained behavior, transfer tasks, and longer-term retention?
   - What populations, dosages, contexts, and limitations apply?

5. Boundary conditions
   - When does an apparent lack of discipline reflect environment, incentives, task design, sleep, stress, mental state, skill deficits, or opportunity costs rather than a stable individual characteristic?
   - What evidence supports or contradicts claims that self-discipline is broadly generalizable across domains?

## Inclusion principles

Prefer:
- peer-reviewed primary research
- systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- preregistered or otherwise methodologically transparent studies
- validated measurement studies
- longitudinal and experimental designs when answering development/causality questions

Record study design, population, sample size when available, measures, comparison condition, outcome, uncertainty, limitations, and provenance.

## Exclusion / caution

Do not treat:
- popular advice
- influencer claims
- unsourced frameworks
- commercial training claims
- AI-generated assertions

as scientific evidence.

They may be recorded separately as hypotheses or opinions if relevant.

## Required final research outputs

HDS should produce, in order:

1. Evidence map
2. Definition map
3. Construct/component map
4. Measurement map
5. Development/intervention evidence map
6. Contradiction and uncertainty map
7. Evidence-backed findings
8. Claims with explicit provenance and scientific classification
9. Knowledge gaps
10. New bounded research questions

The final answer must distinguish established evidence from preliminary evidence, inference, hypothesis, and unresolved questions.

## First operational milestone

The first milestone is NOT to answer the question.
It is to build a traceable evidence base capable of answering it.

No scientific claim should be promoted merely because an agent produced a plausible synthesis.
